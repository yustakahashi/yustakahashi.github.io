About

I am a psychiatrist and doctoral researcher at the University of Tokyo. My work examines how heterogeneity in psychiatric disorders emerges across brain and body, life history, clinical course, and lived experience, and how these differences can inform treatment and support.

I combine neuroimaging, clinical databases, and natural language processing with questions arising from clinical practice and patient and public involvement.

Research

Biological heterogeneity

I use large-scale, multi-site neuroimaging data and data-driven methods to study individual differences in brain structure and function across schizophrenia and related psychiatric conditions.

Life history, lived experience & clinical course

I study childhood adversity, psychopathology, treatment response, and clinical trajectories using clinical databases and electronic health records, including natural language processing.

Clinical practice & co-production

My clinical and research interests include consultation-liaison psychiatry, recovery-oriented care, psychodynamic psychiatry, rare conditions such as 22q11.2 deletion syndrome, and patient and public involvement.
