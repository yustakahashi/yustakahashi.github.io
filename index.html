About

I am a psychiatrist and researcher at the University of Tokyo. My research examines how heterogeneity in psychiatric disorders emerges across the brain and body, life history, clinical course, and lived experience, and how these differences can be connected to treatment and support.

I combine neuroimaging, large-scale clinical data, and natural language processing with questions arising from clinical practice and patient and public involvement.

Research

01

Biological heterogeneity

Large-scale neuroimaging and data-driven methods to characterize individual differences in brain structure and function across schizophrenia and related psychiatric conditions.

02

Life history & clinical trajectories

Childhood adversity, psychopathology, treatment response, and longitudinal clinical information studied through clinical databases and electronic health records.

03

Lived experience & clinical practice

Connecting quantitative findings with subjective experience, recovery-oriented care, psychodynamic psychiatry, and patient and public involvement.
