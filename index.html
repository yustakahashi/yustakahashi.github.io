About

I am a psychiatrist and doctoral researcher at the University of Tokyo. My research focuses on psychiatric heterogeneity across multiple levels, including brain structure, developmental and environmental exposures, clinical course, and patients' own accounts of illness and recovery.

Alongside quantitative neuroimaging and clinical data analysis, I am interested in how psychiatric research can better connect biological findings with treatment decisions, clinical narratives, and lived experience.

Research

Neuroimaging & heterogeneity

Structural MRI and data-driven approaches to characterize variation within schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and related clinical phenotypes.

Development & adversity

Neurodevelopmental trajectories, childhood adversity, and the ways developmental and environmental factors relate to later psychiatric outcomes.

Clinical records & NLP

Natural language processing and large-scale clinical records to study treatment response, symptoms, life-course information, and clinical decision-making.

Lived experience & clinical meaning

Approaches that connect quantitative findings with patient-reported experience, narrative, and co-production in psychiatric research.
